# Independent ICLR scientific review: v1 novelty and significance

Reviewed 19 September 2026. Scope: current `submission/v1` main text, theory, related work, experimental descriptions and relevant appendix proofs; primary literature checked directly. No uncompleted follow-up results are credited. No original research files were edited and no GPU work was performed. This is a scientific review, not a PDF layout audit or an independent reproduction of numerical results.

**Overall: 4/10, reject. Confidence: 8/10.** The manuscript is candid and substantially more defensible than a broad claim that JEPAs inherently fail under stochasticity. Its exposition and claim discipline are stronger than its research contribution. As submitted, the main new empirical result is an incompletely isolated failure of one small-domain imagination pipeline. The formal contribution adds a narrow measurable-coding counterexample to an already known nuisance optimum. Neither yet supplies a sufficiently distinctive explanation or general empirical finding for a clear ICLR acceptance.

These are independent scientific judgments, not a requested target rating. I would separate **exposition/claim discipline: 8/10**, **formal correctness within stated assumptions: 8/10**, **experimental identification: 4/10**, **novelty: 3/10**, and **significance: 4/10**. These dimensions are not averaged mechanically into the overall recommendation.

## Central rejection risk

The submission's own careful qualifications remove the stronger causal interpretation without replacing it with a comparably strong positive contribution. The gate result does not isolate dynamics, state omission, stochastic averaging, or geometric scale as the cause of control failure. The theory explicitly does not explain the gate. The conclusion that encoding, prediction and downstream control need separate evaluation is sensible but substantially anticipated by prior JEPA and model-based RL work.

This is not a request to restore overclaims. It is a request for new evidence that answers one remaining scientific question decisively.

## What is already known, checked against primary sources

| Prior work | Material overlap and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| [Sobal et al. 2022, §2 and §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.10831#S2) | Explicitly constructs normally distributed episode-static nuisance with an identity predictor, yielding zero prediction loss while ignoring the moving object. Its evaluation already probes both encoder outputs and predicted representations. Proposition 1 is a valid specialization to SIGReg plus a control-equivalence statement, not a new failure mechanism. |
| [LeWorldModel, Appendix F.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v3#A6.SS2) | Explicitly reports good TwoRoom physical-state probes despite weaker planning and identifies dynamics/planning as possible remaining causes. The paper therefore cannot claim discovery of decodable-but-poor-control JEPA representations. |
| [LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3) | Its distributional regularization and downstream-probe analysis are not a guarantee that every control-relevant variable survives an arbitrary temporal prediction task. The present Gaussian counterexamples do not refute the regularizer's distribution-matching property. The new theorem concerns missing task-geometry assumptions, not an error in Gaussian matching. |
| [Terver et al., §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v4#S6) | Already identifies deterministic MSE prediction with conditional means of potentially multimodal futures, and discusses stochastic extensions. Its frozen visual encoders differ from the jointly trained encoder studied here. The present manuscript correctly avoids treating the conditional-mean observation as new. |
| [PSG-JEPA, §4.4–4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06799#S4.SS4) | Explicitly separates frozen-latent goal-conditioned control from open-loop predictor evaluation, and studies physical-state recoverability and policy learning. Its physical-state supervision is an important distinction, but does not remove its direct overlap with the proposed evaluation decomposition. It is absent from v1's related work. |
| [Temporal-Distance-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25337v2#S1) | Directly formulates a gap between latent prediction and planning-relevant geometry, and separates using a learned cost from shaping the representation while retaining geometric planning. It does not establish stochasticity-induced information deletion, but further reduces the novelty of a generic prediction/control mismatch narrative. |
| [TD-JEPA, Bagatella et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00739) | This is temporal-difference JEPA, distinct from Temporal-Distance-JEPA. Its primary abstract describes policy-conditioned long-term prediction and state/task encoders for zero-shot reward optimization. It is useful scope-setting evidence that reward-free latent prediction is not restricted to one-step Euclidean goal planning. I did not independently audit its full proofs. |
| [Gulati and Nemenman 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07770#S3) | Already studies slow-nuisance bias and changes the contrastive sampling mechanism, with noise-timescale and trajectory-length interventions. Its contrastive objective differs from SIGReg, but a new nuisance study needs an equally precise identified mechanism. |
| [Physically Grounded JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03565v1#S1) | Explicitly states that the Gaussian prior does not specify which physical variables to retain; adds state-alignment and inverse-dynamics supervision. It also reports that temporal straightening can coexist with a smaller transition subspace and weaker planning. This complementary intervention literature is missing from v1. |

An additional primary abstract worth incorporating is [Predictive but Not Plannable: RC-aux](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07278), which frames local-prediction versus long-horizon reachability mismatch and evaluates LeWM continuation and matched-from-scratch variants. I checked its abstract, not its full experimental implementation; it should not carry a stronger claim than that in this review.

I did not find a directly identical published construction for Proposition 2 in this targeted review. That is not a proof of priority across all measure-theoretic representation-learning literature. The exact construction can be credited as a modest technical contribution without making it carry the entire paper.

## Formal correctness versus scientific consequence

**Proposition 1 is valid under its explicit assumptions.** Nonnegative prediction loss and a population regularizer that vanishes for an exactly Gaussian nuisance law give the claimed zero-loss optimum. The deployment coupling correctly allows the nuisance draw to influence subsequent states through the controller's actions. The distinction between population and finite-batch SIGReg is valuable. However, the essential static-Gaussian/identity construction is already in Sobal 2022; changing the anti-collapse functional does not create a new mechanism (`sections/theory.tex:18–33`).

**The innovation lower bound also appears valid, but assumes the property whose scale is at issue.** The uniform lower Lipschitz bound is far stronger than injectivity or control sufficiency. It establishes a cost for uniformly state-resolving encoders under fresh conditional innovations; it does not show that trained encoders delete state, and persistent hidden wind is not automatically a fresh innovation (`sections/appendix.tex`, Corollary “Innovation cost”).

**Proposition 2 checks out mathematically.** The conditional Gaussian quantile construction yields the exact joint Gaussian marginal, an almost-sure inverse for bit and nuisance, arbitrarily close state pairs, and the stated conditional-variance loss bound (`sections/appendix.tex:138–170`). Its useful content is that marginal Gaussianity plus exact information preservation does not impose a decision-relevant distance scale.

**Proposition 2 does not establish a Bayes-mean control failure.** The reward example compares observing the current real bit with being state-blind. Every predictor is unable to know the next independent bit. A controller that decodes each current real latent remains optimal even if its dynamics model is the Bayes mean. The exact decoder is discontinuous at within-bin medians and has no finite global Lipschitz constant. Moreover, this example's optimal state value is constant at 1; the sensitive object is the action-conditioned reward/Q-function or the policy, not necessarily the optimal state-value function. These facts limit the relevance of the subsequent generic Lipschitz value bound. The text already acknowledges the main limitation at `sections/theory.tex:57–59`, so this is a significance issue rather than a hidden contradiction.

The standard Lipschitz/Cauchy–Schwarz inequality is correct, but it supplies no estimated sensitivity, finite-network guarantee, or tested causal bridge to the gate (`sections/theory.tex:61–71`). The formal results and empirical observations should remain visibly distinct.

## Empirical contribution and remaining ambiguity

1. **The gate offers the most promising specific observation:** a predictable wall is recovered from real encodings but disappears under the same decoder on predicted latents across three encoder fits. The shared-wall diagnostic usefully excludes a simple pixel-space mixture explanation. However, a decoder trained only on real encodings can fail off-distribution; the manuscript correctly does not claim that predicted latents have lost all wall information (`sections/results.tex:73–85`). Training a separate diagnostic on predicted latents is an important missing test.

2. **The strongest retention/control demonstration and the imagination failure use different interfaces.** Two decoded-latent CNN runs succeed, both on encoder 1. The imagined policies use an MLP, and the matched real-latent MLP also has no discoveries. The existing comparison cannot show that a controller capable of exploiting the retained wall would succeed with real transitions and fail specifically because of learned dynamics. The training protocols additionally change starts, lengths, rewards and termination estimates (`sections/results.tex:4–17`, `35–48`; `sections/controllers.tex`). Reporting these differences honestly does not resolve them.

3. **The known reward/termination weakness is a serious alternative explanation, not a minor nuisance.** Predicted wall-death reward is about −0.05 instead of −1, and wall-death termination AUC is 0.65. A pipeline with that signal can fail even with otherwise useful representation/dynamics. The residual predictor's lower MSE without better policy control is therefore not a clean test of whether stochastic prediction helps (`sections/results.tex:87–92`).

4. **The gray-noise factorial result does not support the principal nuisance-optimum story.** Persistent backgrounds permit subpixel readout in the replicated grid experiment, while per-frame noise plus wind fails with high prediction loss. This is a worthwhile architecture/data interaction, but it neither realizes the low-loss static-nuisance optimum nor distinguishes loss-driven omission from poor optimization. The manuscript says this correctly; it therefore needs another substantive interpretation or should remain exploratory (`sections/cubes.tex`).

5. **Craftax is motivating rather than decisive evidence.** Nearby position matching and 100-step reward control are materially different tasks, and the latter has already-poor head generalization even without transition rollout. One fitted checkpoint cannot establish a general JEPA limitation. This section is well scoped but contributes little new explanation (`sections/craftax.tex`).

6. **Replication is reported responsibly but remains limited.** Twenty-seven imagined policies are nine policies under each of three predictor variants over only three encoder fits. The flow reference has one dynamics fit and only one successful policy. These are useful feasibility/variability observations, not a reliable family ranking. I give credit for separating these levels and retaining final checkpoints.

## Attainable improvements

**Without new training:** explicitly position the paper as a controlled case study rather than an original three-interface methodology; add PSG-JEPA and physical-grounding/RC-aux context; describe Proposition 1 as a specialization of Sobal's construction; clarify that Proposition 2's control contrast is feedback versus blindness, not failure of the conditional mean; label the Lipschitz statement's sensitive object appropriately. Concentrate the main paper on the gate and one genuinely informative cube contrast. These changes improve accuracy and focus, but would not alone move my current recommendation to acceptance.

**Highest-priority experimental upgrade:** a fixed controller interface and fixed PPO protocol with learned versus oracle transition and signal interventions, replicated over independent models. An interpretable result would identify whether dynamics, reward/termination, or their interaction prevents control while the same controller succeeds in its oracle condition. This would convert an anecdotal complete-pipeline failure into knowledge about the specific bottleneck. The intervention must define consistent oracle signals on its transition trajectories; merely assigning real-trajectory rewards to unrelated imagined states is not a valid oracle.

**Best next mechanism test after locating the bottleneck:** a prespecified fully differentiated versus target-stop-gradient comparison under deterministic versus genuinely fresh stochastic dynamics, with the same architecture, data size, updates, SIGReg and seed schedule. Track position readout and position-specific counterfactual latent sensitivity throughout training, along with prediction loss and regularization. This tests whether the target branch produces a stochasticity-specific loss of accessible state information. A direct source check confirms that the existing wind process contains fresh independent draws at every transition (`studies/redcube/env.py:84–87`), so it is not merely a hidden episode-constant velocity; survival-conditioned evaluation still requires care. A positive endpoint alone could still reflect optimization; the learning trajectories and an explicit interaction are more informative. A null result should reject this proposed explanation for the tested recipe. This is more distinctive than another generic prediction-versus-control table, but it is not yet a completed contribution.

**Theory upgrade:** a finite-resolution or regularity-controlled result connecting a retained feature's geometric scale to a restricted readout's error or sample requirement would be more consequential than unrestricted measurable decoding. An actual finite-horizon action-ranking counterexample would need a fully specified planner and reward model, and a matched stochastic/expectation comparison. Such results must be derived, not inferred from the fresh-bit example.

I would reconsider the score if matched interventions identify a reproducible failure mechanism that is not already explained by head fitting or controller optimization, with an explicit boundary condition or successful targeted correction. More polished figures, more unpaired policy seeds, or stronger language about Gaussianity would not address the central concern.
