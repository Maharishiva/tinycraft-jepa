\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A world model can retain observable state without supporting a policy trained in imagination.
We study this distinction in a JEPA trained with squared next-latent prediction and a Gaussian
marginal regularizer, without reconstruction or reward supervision during representation learning
\citep{maes2026lewm,balestriero2025lejepa}. Non-collapse constrains the distribution of features;
it does not specify which features matter for control or how accurately imagination must preserve them.

Our gate task makes this distinction observable. A moving wall approaches a controlled cube,
but reveals its gap too late for an agent waiting at the centre to respond. Survival requires
retreating before the reveal and then moving toward the gap. Real frames encoded and decoded through
the frozen JEPA retain the wall; decoded predicted rollouts lose it (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}).


The primary comparison holds the frozen encoders and MLP torso design fixed. Policies trained on
real transitions pass $0.94$ walls per episode, versus $0.27$ for policies trained inside the JEPA.
The latter use learned transitions, rewards and terminations, and a different PPO collection and
update protocol. The contrast measures the resulting pipeline deficit; it does not locate its cause.
The same MLP design on true pixels also fails to discover the strategy.

A convolutional policy on decoded frozen latents discovers the strategy in $2/9$ runs, both on
encoder $1$. At least one fitted representation thus supports successful control with decoder supervision.
A single flow-model checkpoint supplies a feasibility reference under the same data and model-training
budget, without establishing a ranking between model families.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We establish a measured deficit of the tested imagination-and-training
pipeline, an existence result for useful information in its frozen representation, and population
counterexamples to control sufficiency under its objective. The counterexamples separate Gaussian
non-collapse from state information, and exact decodability from prediction-loss scale. They do not
explain the empirical failure: neither the geometric diagnostic nor residual sampling identifies a cause.
