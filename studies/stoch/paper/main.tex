\lhead{Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027}

\begin{abstract}
A joint-embedding predictive world model (JEPA) learns its latent by predicting its own next latent and
keeping the latent marginal Gaussian, with no decoder and no reward. We ask what such a model keeps when
survival depends on one thing: a moving wall whose gap is revealed late. The JEPA imagines a cube in an
empty room. Its encoder keeps the wall: an off-budget decoder renders it from real encodings, and a
convolutional policy trained on those decoded frames learns the retreat-and-wait strategy in two of nine
runs, as it does on true pixels in two of three. Its imagination loses it: with the same MLP torso, PPO
trained inside the JEPA passes $0.27$ walls per episode over $27$ runs, against $0.94$ when trained in the
real gate on the JEPA's own frozen encoders. The deficit belongs to the imagination pipeline as a whole,
predictor, fitted reward and termination heads and PPO protocol together; sampling the fitted residual
predictor does not remove it. A flow model trained on the same data and budget keeps the wall. We prove
that the objective permits this: a non-collapsed global optimum can carry no state at all, and an exactly
Gaussian, fully decodable latent can hold a control-relevant feature at arbitrarily small amplitude.
Non-collapse does not guarantee control-relevant state, and decodable state does not guarantee that
imagination uses it.
\end{abstract}
