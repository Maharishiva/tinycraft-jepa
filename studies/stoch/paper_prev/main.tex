\lhead{Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027}

\begin{abstract}
We study a joint-embedding predictive world model (JEPA) trained with LeWM's objective, squared
next-latent prediction plus a Gaussian marginal regularizer, no decoder and no reward, in a world where
survival depends on one thing: a moving wall whose gap is revealed late. On the same data and ten-minute
budget a generative flow model imagines the wall and commits to a gap; the grid JEPA imagines an empty
room: the wall's latent signal is below the predictor's one-step residual, decoded rollouts lose the wall
within two steps in all three model seeds, and termination heads barely read wall deaths. PPO with an
MLP torso passes $0.27$ walls per episode after training inside the JEPA ($27$ runs), against $0.94$
after training in the real gate on the JEPA's own frozen encoders (nine runs) and $0.91$ on true pixels;
eight of nine runs inside the flow model pass $0.72$--$1.18$ walls, the ninth $3.95$. The real-gate
comparison changes dynamics, reward and termination at once: it shows a deficit of the imagination
pipeline, predictor and fitted heads together, and does not isolate the predictor. The wall the pipeline
loses is decodable: a convolutional policy trained on the frozen latent decoded back to pixels learns the
retreat-and-wait strategy in two of nine runs, as it does on true pixels in two of three. Sampling the
fitted residual predictor does not improve control, and amplifying the wall's latent signal for the MLP
policy produces no discovery. We prove that the objective allows this: a non-collapsed global optimum
can carry no state at all, and an exactly Gaussian, fully decodable latent can hold a control-relevant
feature at arbitrarily small amplitude. Non-collapse does not guarantee control-relevant state, and
decodable state does not guarantee that imagination uses it.
\end{abstract}

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}
The worlds, models, controllers and every diagnostic are JAX programs; the base world-model comparison uses a ten-minute budget on one A100 including compilation, the fixed-step sweeps report their times, and the off-budget heads, decoders and policies have the budgets given in Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}. Section~\ref{sec:controllers} states the evaluation protocol; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}
the metrics. All numbers in the text are produced by one script from the run ledgers. Code, data generation and
the run ledgers will be released as an anonymous artifact.

\subsubsection*{Statement on the use of AI}
Language models were used to draft and edit prose, to check the proofs for gaps, to write analysis and plotting
scripts, and to act as an adversarial reviewer of the manuscript. Every experiment, number, proof and claim was
specified, run and verified by the authors.
