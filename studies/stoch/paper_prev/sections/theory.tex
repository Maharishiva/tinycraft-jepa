\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

We separate representation sufficiency from planning with a point prediction; these are population
statements under explicit function-class assumptions and do not describe optimization trajectories.

\subsection{A non-collapsed, control-insufficient global optimum}
\label{sec:theory-rep}

For a latent law on $\R^D$, define the population regularizer
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:popsigreg}
\mathrm{SIGReg}_\infty(\mathcal L(z))=
\int_{\mathbb S^{D-1}}\!\int_0^3 w(t)
\left[\big(\E\cos(t\langle z,u\rangle)-w(t)\big)^2
+\big(\E\sin(t\langle z,u\rangle)\big)^2\right]dt\,d\sigma(u),
\end{equation}
where $w(t)=e^{-t^2/2}$ and $\sigma$ is uniform on the sphere.
This nonnegative functional vanishes for $z\sim\Nrm(0,I_D)$; we use only this direction, and the
finite-sample regularizer differs from it by an estimator bias (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

Assume \textbf{(A1)} observations have form $x_t=R(p_t,b)$, and a measurable map $\beta$ recovers the episode-constant nuisance $b$.
Under the training distribution, $b$ is independent of the controlled trajectory and actions.
For deployment, assume $b$ is independent of the initial controlled state and all exogenous transition randomness.
Controlled dynamics and rewards do not otherwise depend on $b$.
Assume \textbf{(A2)} a measurable map $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi(b)\sim\Nrm(0,I_D)$.
Assume \textbf{(A3)} the encoder class contains $\varphi\circ\beta$, and the predictor class contains the last-latent copy map.
These realizability assumptions are substantive; finite pixel textures need not satisfy them exactly.

\begin{proposition}[Texture trap]
\label{prop:representation}
Under (A1)--(A3), an encoder and predictor attain zero population prediction loss and zero $\mathrm{SIGReg}_\infty$.
Their latent has covariance $I_D$ and satisfies $I(z_t;p_{1:T})=0$ under the training distribution.
Any deployed controller observing only these latents, time, and its own actions has the value of a randomized state-blind policy, at most the supremum over such policies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $f^\star=\varphi\circ\beta$ and let $g^\star$ copy its last latent.
Then $z_t=\varphi(b)=z_{t+1}$, so every prediction residual vanishes.
The Gaussian marginal gives covariance $I_D$ and zero population regularizer.
Both objective terms are nonnegative, so their sum is globally minimized at zero.
Training-distribution independence gives $I(\varphi(b);p_{1:T})=0$.

For deployment, draw the nuisance $B$ and the controller's random seed $U$ at reset, independently of
the initial state and of the transition randomness; run the original controller in the environment
with nuisance $B$, and let the state-blind policy, with private seed $(B,U)$, feed the constant history
$\varphi(B)$ to a copy of that controller and reproduce its actions with $U$. Coupled to the same
initial state and transition randomness, the two policies choose the same first action, and if their actions agree through step $t$ their states,
latent histories and seeds agree, so their next actions agree; by induction trajectories, returns and
terminations coincide. Nothing here assumes independence between $b$ and states influenced by its
induced actions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An innovation cost for state-resolving encoders]
\label{cor:strict}
Let $p_{t+1}=p_t+\mu(a_t)+w_t$ and $\mathcal H_t=\sigma(p_{\le t},b,a_{\le t})$.
Write $\Sigma_t=\Cov(w_t\mid\mathcal H_t)$ and assume finite second moments for states and encoded observations.
Assume observations have the same form $x_t=R(p_t,b)$ with fixed nuisance $b$.
Suppose $\|f(R(p,b))-f(R(p',b))\|\ge\kappa\|p-p'\|$ for all admissible $p,p',b$, with $\kappa>0$.
For every predictor using latent and action history,
$\E\|g(z_{\le t},a_{\le t})-f(x_{t+1})\|^2 \ge \kappa^2\E\tr\Sigma_t$ (Equation~\ref{eq:innovation}
in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). If $\E\tr\Sigma_t>0$, this exceeds the texture encoder's zero prediction loss under (A1)--(A3).
\end{corollary}

The bound is the innovation after conditioning on state history and nuisance, so a persistent wind is
not automatically innovation, and it covers encoders that preserve every state distance at a uniform
scale; Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} proves it without a conditional-independence assumption on the wind.

\subsection{Planning with a mean}
\label{sec:theory-plan}

\begin{lemma}[Squared prediction loss targets a conditional mean]
\label{lem:mean}
Let $\E\|z'\|^2<\infty$.
For a fixed encoder and data law, over all measurable square-integrable predictors of $z'$ from the latent history $c$ and action $a$, the unique squared-error minimizer is $m(c,a)=\E[z'\mid c,a]$, up to null sets, and for every goal $z_\star$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomposition}
\E[\|z'-z_\star\|^2\mid c,a]
=\|m(c,a)-z_\star\|^2+\tr\Cov(z'\mid c,a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof is the orthogonal decomposition in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The second term is what a
goal-image planner with a mean predictor drops; it is action-independent only in special cases
(Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}).

\begin{proposition}[Certainty-equivalent planning can incur maximal regret]
\label{prop:regret}
There exists a one-step problem with two actions and reward range $[-1,0]$ where exact conditional-mean planning incurs regret $1$.
Here the planner chooses $a_{\mathrm{CE}}\in\arg\max_a r(\E[y\mid a])$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $S=[-2,-1]\cup[1,2]$, with $r(y)=0$ on $S$ and $r(y)=-1$ elsewhere. Action $A$ produces
$y=\mp3/2$ and action $B$ produces $y=0$ or $3$, each outcome with probability $1/2$: $\E[r(y)\mid A]=0$ and
$\E[r(y)\mid B]=-1$, the means are $0$ and $3/2$, the scores $r(0)=-1$ and $r(3/2)=0$, so the mean
planner selects $B$ with regret $1$, the whole reward range. Both outcome variances equal $9/4$.
\end{proof}

An exact expected-reward head solves this one-step problem without sampling; the proposition concerns the specified score, not every controller with a deterministic predictor. The boundary is classical: under affine dynamics with additive noise whose joint law is the same for every open-loop action sequence, and quadratic costs, an open-loop sequence costs its mean rollout plus a sequence-independent constant, so mean rollouts rank sequences exactly \citep{simon1956dynamic,theil1957note} (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Survival with termination and a $\{-1,0\}$
reward is not an instance, and for nonlinear dynamics iterated one-step means need not be multi-step means.

\subsection{Decodable information can have arbitrarily small amplitude}
\label{sec:amplitude}

\begin{proposition}[Small amplitude under an exactly Gaussian marginal]
\label{prop:amplitude}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ and integer $D\ge1$, there is an observation process $x_t=R(p_t,b)$ with episode-constant nuisance $b$.
Its state $p_t\in\{0,1\}$ is a fresh fair bit, drawn independently of $b$, previous states, and previous actions.
There is a measurable encoder $f_\varepsilon$ with $z_t\sim\Nrm(0,I_D)$ from which $p_t$ and $b$ are exactly recoverable almost surely.
For the same nuisance, the two state encodings satisfy
$0<\|f_\varepsilon(R(1,b))-f_\varepsilon(R(0,b))\|<2\varepsilon$.
Over all square-integrable measurable predictors of latent and action history through $t$, the optimal
next-latent prediction is their midpoint $m(b)$, with $0<\Lcal_{\mathrm{pred}}(f_\varepsilon,m)\le\varepsilon^2$,
$I(m(b);p_{t+1})=0$ and $\mathrm{SIGReg}_\infty(\mathcal L(z_t))=0$.
The state is control-relevant: with actions $a_t\in\{0,1\}$ and reward $\mathbf1\{a_t=p_t\}$, latent
feedback earns $1$ per step and every state-blind policy $1/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Cut $\R$ into intervals of width $2\varepsilon$ and let the nuisance pick an interval with its Gaussian
mass and a uniform position inside it; let the fair bit decide whether that position lands in the lower
or the upper half of the interval's conditional Gaussian mass. The first latent coordinate is
then exactly $\Nrm(0,1)$, the bit is read off from the half, the two encodings of the bit lie in one
interval, and the optimal predictor is their midpoint, which depends on the nuisance only and costs at
most $\varepsilon^2$. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} gives the construction in full.
\end{proof}

The construction is measurable, with no finite-network or robust-decoding guarantee; it averages a
fresh innovation and does not by itself explain the deletion of a predictable wall.
