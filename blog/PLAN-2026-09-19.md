# Research + content plan, revision 2 (2026-09-19, after Hamming and Schulman)

Scope agreed with Fred: black-and-white noise only (per frame / per episode), no noise, wind / no wind,
plus whatever is needed to make the stochasticity argument actually testable. Deliverable: a blog post
(RU) whose evidence is control, not probes. Revision 1 is in git history of this file; what changed and
why is section 0.

## 0. What the two essays changed

Hamming: work on a problem that matters and has an attack; "never solve an isolated problem except as
characteristic of a class"; state the problem clearly; keep the contrary evidence; sell the picture
before the details; make it something others can stand on. Schulman: goal-driven, general solutions only,
aim for the 10x not the 10 %, one vertical slice before any fan-out, finish one deliverable instead of
the zoo of half-done arms, keep the ledger, and reimplement to known numbers before trusting a port.

Applied here:

1. **The problem, stated once.** "A world model is used to choose actions in a world where the future is
   a distribution. Which property decides whether that works: the representation objective, or the
   deterministic mean predictor?" LeWM is the case study, not the target. The result must be about the
   two classes (predictability-trained joint encoders; deterministic MSE predictors), so it carries to
   V-JEPA-style planners, DINO-WM, PLDM, and to our own rover models.
2. **Isolate the two mechanisms with a 2x2, not a model zoo.** Revision 1 compared three whole models
   (LeWM cls, JEPA grid, flow) so any difference had two causes at once (encoder objective *and*
   predictor type). Both cross cells are cheap because `tile_factored` and the tinywmcraft tokenizer
   produce the **same 4x4x32 latent shape**, so the flow dynamics trainer runs on JEPA latents unchanged
   and the factored MSE predictor runs on frozen flow latents unchanged:

   | | deterministic MSE predictor | flow-matching predictor |
   |---|---|---|
   | JEPA-trained encoder (predictability + SIGReg) | LeWM / `tile_factored` (exists) | **new**: flow predictor on frozen JEPA latents |
   | reconstruction-trained encoder (tinywmcraft tokenizer) | **new**: MSE predictor on frozen flow latents | tinywmcraft flow WM (exists) |

   Premise 1 (dropped variance) is the column difference on a fixed row. Premise 2 (useless latent) is
   the row difference on a fixed column. Each new cell is a predictor-only training of ~5 min.
3. **The decisive experiment is minimal and holds everything else fixed.** Same latent (reconstruction
   row), same planner (goal-image MPC), same cost, same start states and noise; the only difference is
   mean rollout vs sampled rollouts with the expected cost. If the mean planner dashes into the wall and
   the sampled one does not, premise 1 is demonstrated with nothing else in the way. Only after that do
   the applied versions (the paper's LeWM planner; PPO inside each model) get reported.
4. **The world must contain the failure or the test is empty.** The current cube world has
   action-independent wind variance and linear dynamics, so a mean planner ranks actions exactly like
   the true model (revision 1, section 3). The `dash` world adds action-dependent variance; it is the
   preamble's `{+1, -1}` vs `0.1` example in pixels. Without it we would be publishing a representation
   study and calling it a stochasticity study.
5. **Vertical slice first.** Hour one runs the entire pipeline on one world (`dash`) and produces the
   final-format figure. Only then fan out to the six noise worlds. In revision 1 every stage ran across
   all worlds before the next stage started, so a bug in the last stage would surface at 5 a.m.
6. **Close the zoo.** The 6 architecture arms, batch arms, whitening variants, decoder study, Craftax
   `tile_factored`, `dash_wind` and the 500k retrain of the black worlds are out of tonight's scope.
   They were exploration; the deliverable is one finished matrix with pre-registered predictions.
7. **Pre-register and keep the contrary evidence.** Section 5 writes the expected result per cell
   before anything runs, and the post reports every cell including the ones where JEPA is fine.
8. **Make it reusable.** The two worlds and the scoring script become a one-file test
   (`studies/redcube` already is); the post ends with "run your world model through this". The
   risk-calibration curve is a general diagnostic for any world model, not a cube trick.
9. **A port needs a known number.** We never reproduced a LeWM paper number. Out of scope tonight; the
   black-background success and the passing port tests are the honest substitute, said explicitly.

## 2. The two tests

### T1, risk: the `dash` world (premise 1)
Black background, no wind, 500k random-policy transitions, 8 actions: four 3-px steps (deterministic)
and four **dash** moves: 9 px along the axis plus a perpendicular slip of +5 or -5 px with probability
1/2 each. Border death and 128-step cap unchanged. Task: reach the centre and stay alive.
- From 9 px left of the centre `dash right` has mean = centre (mean-model cost 0, true expected cost
  25); three steps reach it exactly. The mean planner dashes; the exact planner steps.
- Along a wall a dash kills with probability 1/2 while its mean path is safe: the absorbing border
  makes the mean rollout wrong about survival, which is the `z^2` flavour of the argument.
- Expected picture: two half-transparent cubes in the mean rollout, one cube in one place per flow
  sample.

### T2, representation: gray noise worlds (premise 2)
`gray_frame_{still,wind}`, `gray_episode_{still,wind}` as trained, plus `off_{still,wind}` as the
"it works" controls. The question per world is only: can a controller built on this latent bring the
cube to the centre?

## 3. Experiment matrix

### 3.1 Cells (models), per world
| cell | encoder | predictor | exists? | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J/M | JEPA (`tile_factored`) | MSE factored | yes | – |
| J/M-cls | LeWM published (`lewm6`) | MSE AR | yes | – |
| J/F | frozen `tile_factored` latents | flow (tinywmcraft `_train_wm` on those latents) | new | ~5 min |
| R/M | frozen tinywmcraft tokenizer latents | MSE factored (our `FactoredPredictor`, no SIGReg) | new | ~5 min |
| R/F | tinywmcraft flow WM | flow | yes, **retrained** with the task reward | 10 min |
`dash` needs J/M, J/M-cls and R/F trained from scratch (30 min) before the cross cells.

### 3.2 Controllers
| id | applies to | method | reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| goal-MPC | all five cells | goal-image latent-distance MPC; exhaustive `8^4`/`4^5` for deterministic predictors; for flow predictors CEM 128 x 4 iters, expected cost over 2 samples, NFE 2, replan every 4 steps (paper style) | none |
| ppo-in-model | all five cells | PPO in 16-step dreams, `rl.py` AC, 150 updates x 512, ~10 min; policy on the latent window; R/F also on decoded pixels (`ppo-px`) | reward head (R/F in-budget, others off-budget `probe.Heads`) + done head as the death penalty |
| real / random / centre | – | PPO in the real world 0.5M steps; uniform; centre controller on the true state | true |

goal-MPC on the flow predictor is the expensive line (~10 min per world at the setting above); it runs
on `dash` and `off_wind` only, where the mean-vs-sampled pair is the point. Elsewhere PPO carries the
generative cells.

### 3.3 Task reward and data
`r = -|pos - centre|_1 / 57` per step (in [-1, 0]) plus `-1` on death; rewritten into every `.npz`
from the stored `pos` (no recollection). R/F is retrained so its in-budget head learns the dense term;
off-budget heads are refit for the other cells (~4 min each). goal-MPC needs no reward at all.

### 3.4 Metrics (real world, 256 episodes x 64 steps, identical start states and noise keys per world)
- settled distance: mean L1 to the centre over steps 17-64 (px); on-target fraction (<= 3 px)
- survival to step 64; deaths per 100 steps
- risk calibration (no controller): 512 held-out contexts x random 8-step plans, imagined `P(death
  within k)` vs true `P(death within k)` from 8 real replays; deterministic cells read the done head on
  the mean rollout, flow cells the fraction of 8 samples that fire the done head
- per cell: mean +- SEM over episodes; model seed 0, plus `tile_factored` seeds 1/2 through goal-MPC in
  the gray worlds so the 0/3 escape result is a control result, not a loss curve
- every run writes a ledger row (world, cell, controller, checkpoint sha, seconds, metrics); all figures
  are generated from the ledger by one script
