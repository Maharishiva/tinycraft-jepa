\section{Craftax under matched data and budget}
\label{sec:craftax}

Craftax-Classic asks the same question at the scale of a game. Both world models are trained on
the same 500,000-transition archive of uniformly random play under the same ten-minute budget, with
the same $4\times4\times32$ latent shape; the same pixel CNN and GRU policy is trained by PPO
inside each for five million imagined steps from the same bank of real episode starts, with
rewards and terminations from each model's own heads and no behavior cloning, action masking or
reward shaping, and evaluated in the real game on 256 episodes. The primary
endpoint is a wooden pickaxe, which needs a multi-step plan (collect wood, place a table, craft) and
cannot be reached by staying alive or wandering (Appendix~\ref{app:inventory}).

Inside the flow model the policy learns the crafting chain: every seed collects wood in every
episode and places a table in over $90\%$, and two of three seeds craft the pickaxe in 242 of 256
episodes; the third, with the seed spread seen in the cube worlds, crafts it in 15
(Table~\ref{tab:inventory}). Inside the JEPA no seed crafts a pickaxe or places a table, wood is
collected in $22\%$, $4\%$ and $2\%$ of episodes, and the achievement count ($0.27$, $0.07$,
$0.02$) falls below uniformly random play ($2.34$) to the level of the no-op policy. These
policies learned what their training environment paid for: the JEPA's reward head paid $0.90$ to
$1.00$ per imagined step, thirty times what the best real policy collects, for behavior the game
rewards at $-0.005$ per step, while the flow's heads paid $0.03$ to $0.06$, the rate of real play.
The cube worlds showed two routes to such a decoupling, a predictor whose outputs leave the region
where the heads were fitted and a mean rollout that averages away the state; this campaign does not
separate them. The same JEPA supports short-horizon goal-directed movement when the goal is an
image a few actions away ($93\%$ of nearby targets reached by open-loop latent planning against
$17\%$ for random actions; Appendix~\ref{app:craftax}). Local state is there; a plan imagined from
it is not.
