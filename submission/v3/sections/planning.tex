\section{Mechanism 2 in the gate world: a kept state that imagination cannot use}
\label{sec:planning}

The gate world is built so that mechanism 1 is off the table. The background is black, the wall is
large and slow, and the only uncertainty is which opening will be revealed. A model that keeps the
state must show the approaching wall; a model that averages over the two openings should still show
the shared solid part. Figure~\ref{fig:gate-filmstrip} shows what the grid JEPA does with a pre-reveal
context: decoded encodings of the \emph{real} future keep the wall and its opening at every step,
and the predictor's own rollout deletes the wall from the first predicted step, in all three
encoder seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:historical-gate}). The flow model's sampled rollout keeps the wall
and commits to one opening. This is the visible signature of the second mechanism: the state was
there, and the mean-trained predictor did not carry it forward.

\paragraph{Why a shared pixel policy.}
Latent-input policies were uninformative in a specific way: a latent-MLP policy trained by PPO
fails the gate even on the \emph{exact} simulator with true rewards (0 of 45 policies above $50\%$
survival; Appendix~\ref{app:matched}), so its failure inside imagination says nothing about the
model. This section therefore uses one pixel CNN and GRU policy for both families, fed by each
model's own decoder, with the same PPO, 150 updates of 512 rollouts of 48 steps, three policy
seeds, final checkpoints and 256 evaluation episodes on shared keys (Section~\ref{sec:worlds}).
The JEPA encoder is model seed 1, the seed on which decoded-latent control had succeeded during
development, and the flow model is one dynamics checkpoint; both facts limit the claim to these
fitted models. The JEPA's reward and termination heads are refitted on all 500,000 training labels
so that head supervision is count-matched to the flow recipe.

\paragraph{Interface qualification.}
Trained on real transitions seen through the JEPA's decoder, all three policy seeds solve the gate:
survival $0.977$, $0.980$ and $0.945$, passing $3.9$ of a possible four walls per episode
(Table~\ref{tab:shared-cnn}, ``real'' rows). The JEPA latent, read by a supervised decoder, carries
everything the task needs, and the policy and optimizer can learn the wait-and-commit strategy from
it. Through the flow decoder two seeds solve it ($0.957$, $0.953$) and one does not ($0.516$): the
flow interface qualifies but is less stable.

\paragraph{Imagination.}
The same policy trained inside the JEPA's imagination, with the same budget and evaluated on the
same 256 episodes, survives $0.027$, $0.008$ and $0.027$ of them and passes $0.55\pm0.28$ walls
per episode. It is not a random policy: it controls for $42$ steps where the blind rules last $24$
and passes the first wall eight times as often as chance. It has learned to avoid the border in a
world without walls. Inside the JEPA's rollouts the fraction of imagined positions still alive rose
from $0.34$ to at most $0.45$ over training while the policy's entropy fell to $0.03$--$0.14$: PPO
converged, confidently, on a strategy the model rewarded and the simulator does not. Inside the
flow model's imagination the same policy survives $0.988$, $0.941$ and $0.977$ of the paired
episodes and passes $3.92\pm0.06$ walls, matching its decoded-real controls. Two facts bound the
reading: the latent fed to the policy in imagination is the one that supported $0.97$ survival from
real transitions, so the deficit is in the predictor's rollout, not the encoder or the interface;
and the flow recipe differs in supervision stage as well as in sampling, so the gap is a recipe gap.
What the comparison establishes is the ordering the paper is about: the state was kept, and a
mean-trained predictor applied to its own outputs did not carry it forward.

\paragraph{What the historical record adds.}
Across the earlier, unmatched protocols, none of 27 policies trained inside the JEPA's imagination
discovered the gate strategy, including nine that used a sampling head fitted on the predictor's
residual and nine that averaged eight of its samples, while policies trained on real transitions
through the same frozen encoders did; the flow reference's historical success was a single policy
of six (Appendix~\ref{app:historical-gate}). Both facts are why this section rests on the matched
comparison rather than on those tables.
