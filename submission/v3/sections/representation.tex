\section{Mechanism 1 in the cube worlds: the state is kept only while it is predictable}
\label{sec:representation}

\paragraph{Design.}
Four gray-noise conditions cross background persistence (per-frame versus persistent) with
dynamics (deterministic versus hidden wind); each receives three independently initialized grid
JEPAs with identical architecture, data and updates. A nonlinear position read-out is fitted on
frozen encodings of training frames, scored on held-out frames as L1 error in pixels, and applied
unchanged to eight-step predicted latents (Appendix~\ref{app:cubes}); a difference between cells is
a difference in what the encoder exposes.

\paragraph{The reversal.}
With deterministic motion the objective keeps the cube in every condition, and per-frame noise is
the \emph{easier} background: $0.115\pm0.012$ pixels against $0.764\pm0.068$ for persistent noise
(Table~\ref{tab:cube-main}, Figure~\ref{fig:cube-readout}). Adding wind flips the ordering and
breaks one cell outright. Under persistent noise the read-out still localizes the cube to
$0.668\pm0.049$ pixels; under per-frame noise it fails at $21.33\pm0.06$ pixels, an error larger
than the cube is wide in every seed. The failure is already present in the encodings of real
frames, before any prediction is made: decoded real encodings from a failing model show a gray
field with at most a faint trace of the cube, and its predicted future is not a scene at all, while
the flow model trained on the same data keeps the cube in both (Figure~\ref{fig:wind-filmstrip}).

\paragraph{Why this is mechanism 1.}
Under deterministic motion the next position is a function of history and action, so keeping it
makes the target predictable and lowers the loss. Wind removes that incentive: the position's
innovations cost prediction loss however the encoder represents them (Corollary~\ref{cor:strict}).
The failing cell is not the zero-loss optimum of Proposition~\ref{prop:representation}: its
prediction loss stays high, about $0.733$ per coordinate, SIGReg is satisfied and the latent is far
from degenerate. The objective found no cheap substitute for the state; it stopped paying for the
state once paying no longer bought predictability. Why persistent noise protects the cube under the
same wind the theory does not decide; the matched design establishes that it does, in all three
seeds.

\paragraph{The state is encoded through the target.}
A second intervention isolates where the incentive acts. In the per-frame conditions we refit
three seeds per world with the prediction target detached from the encoder's gradient, changing
nothing else (the predictor-input and SIGReg gradients remain). With deterministic motion the
detached models lose most of the position information that the joint models keep: mean absolute
read-out error per coordinate $0.37\pm0.05$ pixels joint against $10.93\pm4.90$ detached. With
wind both variants fail identically, $14.51\pm0.12$ against $14.51\pm0.07$ (three seeds each,
paired initialization and data order; Appendix~\ref{app:cubes}). The encoder keeps the cube only
when the joint gradient can make the cube's future predictable, and the wind failure is not a side
effect of that gradient path. Detaching the target, the natural first repair, does nothing here.

\paragraph{Behavioral consequence.}
A policy that cannot see the cube cannot steer it, and a policy trained inside a model that cannot
see the cube fares worse than one that does not look. Table~\ref{tab:shared-cnn} trains one stock
pixel policy by PPO inside each model's imagination, fed by the model's own decoder, for 50 updates
and three policy seeds, and evaluates it on 256 paired episodes (Section~\ref{sec:worlds}). In the
per-frame still world both imaginations produce policies near the ceiling set by blind alternation,
so survival there certifies nothing. In the per-frame wind world every JEPA policy dies in every
episode after $12.5\pm3.1$ steps of control, fewer than a blind random walk lasts; the flow
policies survive $0.945$, $0.129$ and $0.652$ of the episodes, better than their paired JEPA seed
each time, with a seed spread the 50-update budget does not close.
Figure~\ref{fig:imagined-termination} shows what the JEPA policy was trained on: its termination
head, which tracks the recorded rate on encoder latents, reads $0.91$ on every latent the
predictor produces, so an imagined episode ends within two steps whatever the policy does and the
gradient carries no information about actions. The trained policy is arbitrary, and a consistent
arbitrary bias reaches the boundary faster than chance. Under persistent noise the encoder keeps
the cube and the failure changes character: JEPA policies outlast the blind rules ($26.8\pm8.7$
steps) but survive only $0.02\pm0.01$ of episodes, against $0.891$, $0.207$ and $0.137$ for the
flow, and the deterministic rollout that trained them is optimistic rather than dead, keeping $0.40$
of imagined episodes alive at 48 steps where $0.008$ of recorded ones are: the hidden wind averaged
away, the subject of the next section. In the still world the JEPA's imagined reward reaches
$+0.15$ per step though the true reward is never positive: the policy farms the refitted reward
head, a signature that returns, much larger, in Craftax.
