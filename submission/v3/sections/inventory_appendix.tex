\section{Craftax inventory comparison: protocol}
\label{app:inventory}

The protocol behind Section~\ref{sec:craftax}; fixed before the policies were trained
(\texttt{research/craftax\_native\_inventory\_protocol.md} and
\texttt{research/craftax\_inventory\_protocol.md}).

\paragraph{Environment.}
Craftax-Classic-Pixels version 1.5.0 with unchanged defaults: 17 actions, $64\times64$
procedurally generated maps, a 10,000-step native episode limit, 22 achievements. The native reward
is the change in the achievement count plus $0.1$ times the change in health; the evaluator checks
the telescoping identity between summed rewards and terminal achievements and health on every
episode. Termination occurs at the step limit, on lava, or at non-positive health. The evaluator
uses the no-auto-reset environment, stops each episode at its first native termination and freezes
its metrics.

\paragraph{World models.}
Both models are trained on the same 500,000-transition archive of uniformly random play under the
same ten-minute budget with the same $4\times4\times32$ latent shape. The JEPA is the 512-d grid
model with one-step factored predictor and eight-frame history; its pixel decoder and its reward
and termination heads are refitted afterwards on the frozen latent with a fixed recipe (decoder:
8,000 steps, batch 256; heads: 6,000 steps, batch 1,024, hidden 512) whose arguments and resulting
hashes are checked at load time. The flow model is the native recipe with jointly fitted heads.
The frozen model weights of the JEPA are hash-verified to be byte-identical before and after head
fitting.

\paragraph{Policy training in imagination.}
The same pixel CNN and GRU policy as in the cube worlds, with the original Craftax pixel-PPO
settings, is trained for a nominal five million imagined steps (4,998,400 executed) inside each
model from a shared bank of genuine eight-frame episode-start contexts. The JEPA adapter reads
reward and termination from $(z_t,a_t)$, predicts once per action and samples termination from the
head's probability; the flow adapter runs its native expanding-window rollout with four function
evaluations. Imagined episodes are capped at 1,024 steps. No behavior cloning, action masking,
reward alteration, resource injection or checkpoint selection is used; a non-finite loss fails the
run rather than being skipped. Three policy seeds per family, one model seed.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Each final policy is evaluated on 256 native episodes with evaluation seed 123. Environment keys
derive from episode identity and step only, action-sampling keys from a separate stream; the
construction is stored with the results. The policy observes native RGB and its previous action.
Primary endpoint: the fraction of episodes in which the wooden-pickaxe achievement is true at the
end of the episode. Retained: all 22 achievement rates with Wilson intervals, achievement count,
native return, executed steps, survival, termination causes, first-acquisition steps, final
inventory, and the terminal native score where a native termination occurred. Uncertainty
intervals are across evaluation episodes for one fixed policy and do not estimate variation
across model fits.

\paragraph{Reference rows.}
Two state-blind policies are run through the identical evaluator, keys and aggregation: uniform
random actions and the no-op action. Uniform random play reaches one wooden pickaxe in 256
episodes, collects wood in $43\%$ of episodes, places a table in $9\%$ and averages $2.34$
achievements per episode; the no-op policy earns no achievement and a return of $-0.92$ from
health loss. A trained policy below the random row has learned something that hurts.

\paragraph{Imagined reward.}
The training logs record the mean reward per imagined step paid by each model's reward head. In
the real game uniformly random play collects about $0.01$ per step and the best policy of this
campaign $0.03$ (return over executed steps). Both readings are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:inventory}; a policy whose imagined reward exceeds what any real policy collects is
exploiting its reward head rather than the game.
