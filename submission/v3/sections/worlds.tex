\section{Worlds, models and the comparison contract}
\label{sec:worlds}

\paragraph{The red-cube worlds.}
A red $6\times6$ cube moves in a $63\times63$ RGB frame. Four actions move it three pixels left,
up, right or down. Touching the border ends the episode with reward $-1$; all other rewards are
zero and episodes are capped at 128 steps. The background is gray noise, either redrawn every frame
(\emph{per-frame}) or drawn once per episode (\emph{persistent}). Optional \emph{wind} is a hidden
two-dimensional random walk: each coordinate moves by $-1,0,+1$ with probabilities $0.1,0.8,0.1$,
is clipped to $[-2,2]$ and is added to the action displacement. Wind is never observed, so even a
perfect history leaves fresh uncertainty about the next position. Crossing the four gray conditions
with deterministic or wind dynamics gives the matched study of Section~\ref{sec:representation}.

The \emph{gate} world adds a wall that approaches the cube with a single opening at the top or the
bottom, sampled at spawn and revealed only after a fixed delay. Surviving requires retreating,
observing the opening and then committing. The solid part of the wall is common to both futures,
so a model that keeps state but averages over the two openings should still show the wall; a model
that loses the wall has lost state, not just resolved uncertainty in the wrong way.

\paragraph{The JEPA.}
The encoder produces $z_t\in\R^{512}$ arranged as a $4\times4\times32$ grid; a factored
spatial-temporal predictor conditions on eight past latents and actions. Encoder and predictor are
trained jointly with Equation~\ref{eq:lewm}, $\lambda=0.09$, and nothing else. Each model sees
500,000 uniformly random transitions for 8,500 updates, about ten minutes on one A100. Three
independent model seeds are fitted in the matched nuisance study. Pixel decoders, position
read-outs and reward or termination heads are fitted \emph{afterwards} on the frozen latent; their
labels and compute are additional and are reported as such.

\paragraph{The distributional reference.}
The flow recipe uses a reconstruction-trained tokenizer with the same $4\times4\times32$ latent
shape and a causal flow-matching predictor that \emph{samples} the next latent
\citep{lipman2023flowmatching,hafner2025dreamer4}. It is trained on the same transitions under the
same wall-clock budget, with reward and termination heads fitted jointly. The two recipes differ in
more than deterministic versus sampled prediction: the flow tokenizer is supervised by
reconstruction, and its task heads send gradients into its dynamics trunk, whereas the JEPA is
reward-free with post-hoc heads. Every JEPA-versus-flow gap in this paper is therefore a
\emph{recipe} gap. We narrow it where we can, by refitting the JEPA heads on all 500,000 training
labels, and we say so where we cannot.

\paragraph{Behavioral comparison contract.}
Policies are compared through one interface only. Both families expose decoded RGB frames from
their own frozen decoders to the same pixel CNN and GRU policy, trained by the same PPO
implementation with the same optimizer, updates, batch and horizon, and evaluated with the final
checkpoint on 256 stored episodes whose reset, texture and action keys are shared across families
and seeds. Episodes are admitted by surviving a seven-step random prefix, never by policy outcome.
Before any imagination result is read, the same policy is trained on \emph{real} transitions
observed through each model's decoder; a family whose decoded-real control fails has an interface
problem, not an imagination problem. Rewards and terminations inside imagination come from each
model's own heads; no simulator quantity enters training.

\paragraph{Craftax.}
Craftax-Classic \citep{matthews2024craftax} is the consequence outside the toy: 17 actions,
procedurally generated maps, sparse rewards for 22 achievements. Both world models are trained on
the same 500,000-transition archive under the same ten-minute budget, and the same pixel policy is
trained inside each for the same number of imagined steps before native evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:craftax}). Complete settings are in Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}.
