\section{Worlds, models and the comparison contract}
\label{sec:worlds}

\paragraph{The red-cube worlds.}
A red $6\times6$ cube moves in a $63\times63$ RGB frame; four actions move it three pixels.
Touching the border ends the episode with reward $-1$; all other rewards are zero and episodes are
capped at 128 steps. The background is gray noise, either redrawn every frame (\emph{per-frame})
or drawn once per episode (\emph{persistent}). Optional \emph{wind} is a hidden two-dimensional
random walk (each coordinate moves by $-1,0,+1$ with probabilities $0.1,0.8,0.1$, clipped to
$[-2,2]$) added to the action displacement; it is never observed, so even a perfect history leaves
fresh uncertainty about the next position. The \emph{gate} world adds a wall that approaches the
cube with a single opening at the top or the bottom, sampled at spawn and revealed only after a
fixed delay; surviving requires retreating, observing the opening and committing.

\paragraph{The JEPA.}
The encoder produces $z_t\in\R^{512}$ arranged as a $4\times4\times32$ grid; a factored
spatial-temporal predictor conditions on eight past latents and actions. Encoder and predictor are
trained jointly with Equation~\ref{eq:lewm}, $\lambda=0.09$, and nothing else, on 500,000
uniformly random transitions for 8,500 updates, about ten minutes on one A100. Pixel decoders,
position read-outs and reward or termination heads are fitted \emph{afterwards} on the frozen
latent; their labels and compute are additional and reported as such.

\paragraph{The distributional reference.}
The flow recipe uses a reconstruction-trained tokenizer with the same $4\times4\times32$ latent
shape and a causal flow-matching predictor that \emph{samples} the next latent
\citep{lipman2023flowmatching,hafner2025dreamer4}, trained on the same transitions under the same
wall-clock budget with jointly fitted reward and termination heads. The recipes differ in more than
deterministic versus sampled prediction: the flow tokenizer is supervised by reconstruction and its
task heads send gradients into its dynamics trunk, whereas the JEPA is reward-free with post-hoc
heads. Every JEPA-versus-flow gap in this paper is therefore a \emph{recipe} gap.

\paragraph{Behavioral comparison contract.}
Policies are compared through one interface: each family's frozen decoder renders its latents as
RGB for the same pixel CNN and GRU policy, trained by the same PPO with the same budget and
evaluated with its final checkpoint on 256 stored episodes whose reset, texture and action keys
are shared across families and seeds; an episode is admitted by surviving a seven-step random
prefix, never by policy outcome. Before any imagination result is read, the same policy is trained
on \emph{real} transitions seen through each decoder, so an interface failure cannot be mistaken
for an imagination failure. Rewards and terminations in imagination come from each model's own
heads; no simulator quantity enters training (Appendices~\ref{app:hparams} and~\ref{app:shared-cnn}).
