\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A world model can ignore texture while preserving a doorway's position, available opening, and
response to action. Learning such abstractions without a downstream reward is a central attraction
of joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs): their targets are representations of future
observations rather than future pixels \citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa}.
Recent systems demonstrate useful goal-conditioned planning from both pretrained and jointly
learned representations \citep{assran2025vjepa2,sobal2025pldm,maes2026lewm}. The difficult question
is what a particular success or failure establishes about the learned model.

We study this question through interventions on a jointly trained squared-loss JEPA. The model has
a fixed 512-dimensional latent and a Gaussian marginal regularizer. The environments progress from
Craftax to a moving-wall task and then a single cube with controlled nuisance and dynamics. Each
step removes complexity to make a narrower question testable: can the model guide local actions,
can its training environment support a competent controller, and under which conditions does its
representation expose object position?

Craftax supplies a positive result with useful controls. On 384 fresh episodes, nearby position
planning succeeds in $93.2\%$ of open-loop trials and $96.1\%$ with replanning. No-op succeeds in
$35.7\%$, because some targets share the starting position; on the 247 displaced targets, planned
success remains $89.9\%$ and $93.9\%$. Goal generation and execution use independent randomness.
These measurements establish local action-selection capability for the existing checkpoint.

The gate gives a different result. Earlier policies trained in imagination performed worse than
policies trained on real transitions, and decoded predictions lost a visible wall. Those observations
suggested a model limitation, but also changed the task heads and PPO protocol. We therefore train
45 policies under one matched protocol, crossing three frozen models and five policy seeds with
learned transitions, exact transitions with fitted heads, and exact transitions with true signals.
Even the fully exact training environment yields mean survival below $1.5\%$ for every model and
no policy above $50\%$. Exact transitions and signals improve partial progress by $0.12$--$0.22$
walls per episode, but the controller remains unsuccessful. This failure to qualify the common
controller materially changes the interpretation of the original imagination result.

A separate replicated cube study exposes a representation-accessibility boundary. The same grid
recipe supports subpixel position read-out when either noise is persistent or dynamics are
deterministic, while per-frame noise with wind produces large error. Its training loss remains high,
so the empirical failure is distinct from the known low-loss predictable-nuisance construction
\citep{sobal2022slow}. We retain that construction and an exact-Gaussian small-scale counterexample
to state the objective's missing guarantees, rather than use them as explanations of the fitted
networks.

The contribution is the measured scope of these comparisons: a positive local-planning capability,
a matched intervention that prevents a stronger model-failure attribution, and a controlled
nuisance--dynamics interaction in accessible state. Shared protocols, all final outcomes, and model
versus policy replication make the evidence inspectable. The conclusions concern this learning
recipe and its tested interfaces; closely related predictive-learning methods are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:related}.
