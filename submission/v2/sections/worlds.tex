\section{Minimal worlds and model comparison}
\label{sec:worlds}

\paragraph{One object, controlled changes.}
A red $6\times6$ cube moves in a $63\times63$ RGB frame. Four native actions move it three pixels
left, up, right, or down. Contact with the border terminates an episode with reward $-1$; other
rewards are zero, and the cap is 128 steps. Background noise is either redrawn each frame or held
fixed for the episode. Gray noise shares its sample across RGB channels. Optional wind is a hidden
two-dimensional random walk: each coordinate increments by $-1,0,+1$ with probabilities
$0.1,0.8,0.1$, clipped to $[-2,2]$, and is added to the action displacement. This produces fresh
uncertainty even when previous motion is known.

The gate variant adds an approaching wall with a top or bottom opening sampled at spawn and
revealed only after a fixed delay. Retreating gives time to observe the opening and respond.
The common solid part of the wall is present in both possible futures, so its disappearance from
a decoded forecast is an observed model/interface error, not the exact pixel average of the two gaps.

\paragraph{Joint-embedding prediction.}
The grid encoder produces $z_t=f_\theta(x_t)\in\R^{512}$, arranged as $4\times4\times32$ features.
An action-conditioned, factored spatial--temporal predictor uses an eight-frame history. Training
jointly updates encoder and predictor with coordinate-mean squared prediction loss and SIGReg:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lewm}
\Lcal=\frac1D\widehat{\E}\|g_\theta(z_{\le t},a_{\le t})-z_{t+1}\|^2
+\lambda\,\mathrm{SIGReg}(\{z_t\}),\qquad D=512.
\end{equation}
There is no reward or reconstruction target in this representation-learning objective. The gray
conditions share 500,000 training transitions and 8,500 updates, with three independently fitted
JEPA seeds in the matched nuisance study. Decoders and state read-outs are fitted afterward on
frozen encodings; their supervision and compute are additional to the world-model fit.

\paragraph{Distributional reference and behavior.}
The flow recipe uses a reconstruction-trained tokenizer of the same latent shape and a causal
flow-matching predictor \citep{lipman2023flowmatching,hafner2025dreamer4}. It samples future latent
states rather than replacing them by one point. Flow matching itself may use squared vector-field
error; its sampled output distribution is the relevant distinction from a next-state mean.
The model recipes differ in representation supervision, context, and task-head fitting.
Their behavioral comparison therefore uses a shared pixel CNN and GRU, matched policy-training
budgets, native actions/rewards, paired evaluation keys, and final checkpoints. In real evaluation,
each policy observes posterior reconstructions from its own frozen model. Exact-simulator controls
with the same decoded interfaces qualify what each policy can learn before imagination is assessed.
Complete settings, replication levels, and earlier controls are in the appendix.
