\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\lhead{Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027}
\begin{abstract}
Joint-embedding world models learn both which variables to retain and how to predict them. These
choices can fail in different ways: predictable nuisance can displace useful state, and a correct
mean future can omit information needed for a decision. We study these requirements in minimal
redcube worlds, moving from deterministic goal-directed motion to noisy observations and stochastic
dynamics. Two exact examples expose a limitation even under ideal prediction. A planner can rank
actions incorrectly when it evaluates squared goal distance at the mean rather than averaging
outcome costs; recursively applying an exact nonlinear conditional mean can also miss the true
multi-step mean. A known Gaussian nuisance construction gives the corresponding representation-level
failure. In a matched three-seed study, the ranking of background persistence reverses with dynamics:
per-frame noise gives lower position error than persistent noise without wind ($0.115$ versus
$0.764$ pixels), but much higher error with wind ($21.33$ versus $0.668$).
The same latent model supports nearby goal-directed movement, while the unchanged
short-horizon controller degrades sharply on distant visual goals. Exact finite-branch calculations demonstrate small
mean-only action-ranking changes in two frozen encoders, without a demonstrated physical-cost
benefit. The central distinction
is between retaining the state a task needs and retaining the predictive information its decisions
need.
\end{abstract}

The appendices specify model fitting, post-hoc supervision,
evaluation protocols, and replication levels. The tables retain final-checkpoint outcomes rather
than selecting intermediate peaks. The accompanying evidence manifest identifies the source
artifacts used for each result; independent policy seeds are distinguished from independent model
