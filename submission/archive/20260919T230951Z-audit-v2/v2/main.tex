\begin{abstract}
A failed policy trained in a world model can reflect errors in the model, its task heads, or the
controller-training procedure. We examine these alternatives in a jointly trained, squared-loss
JEPA with a fixed 512-dimensional representation. In Craftax, one frozen model reaches $93.2\%$ of
384 nearby goals with six open-loop actions and $96.1\%$ with replanning, versus $35.7\%$ for no-op;
success remains $89.9\%$ and $93.9\%$ on displaced goals. A controlled moving-wall task then tests
whether poor imagined control identifies a model failure. Across 45 matched PPO runs, replacing
learned transitions, rewards, and terminations with exact simulator outcomes improves walls passed
by $0.12$--$0.22$ per episode across three model fits, but none of the 15 exact-environment policies
exceeds $50\%$ survival. Their mean survival remains below $1.5\%$ for every model. The common
controller is itself unsuccessful, so the earlier unmatched real-versus-imagined gap does not
establish a task-solving deficit caused by learned transitions. A replicated nuisance--dynamics study supplies a
separate representation-accessibility failure, while population constructions clarify the limits
of non-collapse guarantees. Together, the measurements distinguish working local planning,
unsuccessful policy training, and inaccessible state under a stated learning recipe.
\end{abstract}

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}
Appendices~\ref{app:hparams}--\ref{app:craftax} specify model fitting, post-hoc supervision,
evaluation protocols, and replication levels. The tables retain final-checkpoint outcomes rather
than selecting intermediate peaks. The accompanying evidence manifest identifies the source
artifacts used for each result; independent policy seeds are distinguished from independent model
fits. Claims based on a single checkpoint are labeled throughout.
