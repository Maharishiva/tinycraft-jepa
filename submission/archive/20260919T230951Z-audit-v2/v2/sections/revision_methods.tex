\subsection{Matched control interventions}
\label{sec:matched-method}
The control comparison fixes the fitted encoder, policy architecture, rollout-start mixture,
context burn-in, optimizer, and update schedule. It changes the source of transitions and task
signals in three conditions: learned latent transitions with fitted reward/termination heads;
exact simulator transitions re-encoded by the same frozen encoder with the same fitted heads;
and exact re-encoded transitions with true rewards and terminations. The actor receives no simulator
state. The primary design crosses three model checkpoints with five policy seeds and all three
conditions. Each policy uses 150 updates of 512 trajectories of length 16, then is evaluated at its
final checkpoint on the same 256 real-episode keys. Intermediate peaks are not selected.

The transition substitution is conditional on terminal semantics: exact simulator trajectories
become absorbing after true termination, while the fitted-head condition can continue to assign
weight and reward to those states. We retain its post-terminal weight and return diagnostics.
Replacing fitted signals under exact transitions is a separate intervention; the effects need not
add independently. A policy interface that fails even with exact transitions and true signals
cannot establish a model-specific control failure. A separate native-grid CNN sensitivity changes
only the policy's inductive bias and is reported without pooling it into the primary MLP results.

\subsection{Read-out transfer between encoded and forecast states}
\label{sec:forecast-method}
We freeze each of the three gate encoders and predictors and fit identical affine ridge read-outs
at horizons 1, 2, 4, and 8. Each read-out is trained on real future encodings, predicted future
latents, or a copied current latent, then evaluated on all three domains. Training and evaluation
contexts come from independently collected archives. The copied-current condition is action
independent and tests how much future-state accuracy can be achieved without predicting a transition.
The targets are cube position, wall presence, and wall position conditioned on true presence.
Simulator labels supervise the diagnostic alone.

We report both predeclared normalization analyses: one common scaler fitted on current training
latents, and separate scalers fitted on each source domain's training latents. A transfer always
uses its source-trained scaler. These transformations affect the diagnostic, not the world model
or policy. Physical-state metrics use a horizon-specific no-reset mask; their sample counts and
selection fractions remain visible. A separate hidden-gap negative control uses all pre-reveal
contexts without future-survival selection, avoiding leakage through selection on the unknown gap.

\subsection{A target-gradient intervention under stochastic dynamics}
\label{sec:target-method}
The objective intervention detaches only the next-latent target in the prediction MSE. It leaves
predictor-input and SIGReg gradients intact, with no teacher network. Thus its forward scalar loss
matches the jointly differentiated condition, while its gradient path differs. The fixed factorial
design crosses these two modes with deterministic or stochastic-wind dynamics under per-frame gray
noise and three model seeds. Both modes in a world/seed pair use identical initialization, sampled
windows, projection directions, optimizer schedule, architecture, and 8,500 updates. Neither reward
nor simulator-state labels enter training, and the latent stays $4\times4\times32$.

The primary endpoint is final-checkpoint position MAE per coordinate from a fixed generic nonlinear
read-out of raw latents. A ridge read-out, prediction loss, variance, and effective rank are secondary.
For each seed, the predeclared interaction is the stop-target benefit in the wind condition minus
its benefit in the deterministic condition. It measures the intervention's dependence on these two
data-generating conditions; wind also changes visitation and episode lengths, so the interaction
alone does not isolate innovation variance. Every admitted final fit is retained, including null
or adverse outcomes.

\subsection{A task-supervised value-aware continuation}
\label{sec:pve-method}
The value-aware experiment keeps the existing encoder, latent dimension, and task heads fixed and
continues only the action encoder and predictor. One arm uses four-step latent MSE; the other adds
a prospectively weighted value-regression term through a frozen teacher trained on realized returns.
An independently fitted auditor is excluded from predictor gradients. Both probes must first pass
a held-out-episode adequacy gate against a constant-return baseline. Failure of this gate ends the
experiment without weakening its threshold. This is a PVE-inspired value-aware continuation
\citep{grimm2021proper}, not exact proper value equivalence or reward-free learning.
